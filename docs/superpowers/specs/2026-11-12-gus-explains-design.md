# Gus Explains — niche change design

Date: 2026-11-12 · Owner: Thimuthu · Status: draft for approval

## 1. Intent and why

The owner is replacing the crypto niche (CryptoFX Daily) with **Gus Explains**: fully automated, daily,
odd-fact cartoon explainer Shorts on the same YouTube channel and Facebook Page.

Why: the reference style (Ricks Clips, e.g. "Levels of Pirate Cannonballs" 9.6M views, "The Ancient Jar
That Warned an Empire" 33M, "Why Perfect Railroads Don't Work" 28M) gets millions of views per Short in a
genre with broad US appeal, while the crypto channel needs constant fresh market data and carries
finance-content risk. The owner wants it hands-off, near-zero cost and eventually monetized (YPP), as before.

Success: two Shorts a day go out on YouTube and Facebook with no owner action; each looks like a moving
cartoon in the reference's visual language (not a slideshow); facts are true and sourced; the channel stays
eligible for YPP.

## 3. Goals, non-goals, anti-goals

**Goals**
- G1. Two Shorts per day, YouTube Short + Facebook Reel, published around **17:00 UTC** (10:30 pm Sri Lanka,
  12:00 pm New York) and **23:00 UTC** (4:30 am SL, 6:00 pm NY). US audience first.
- G2. Fully automatic: topic → research → script → render → upload → log. No human approval step.
- G3. Visual style of the reference video (§5), produced by code, free.
- G4. Every claim true and grounded in sources Gemini found; sources listed in the description.
- G5. Full rebrand: channel name "Gus Explains", handle, profile image, YouTube banner, Facebook cover,
  descriptions — artwork generated by code in the videos' style.
- G6. Crypto features retired cleanly.

**Non-goals**
- Long-form videos, news image posts, market data, trigger engine, lessons, Basics (all retired).
- Hand-animated character acting at the reference's quality (approximated with poses, expressions,
  lip-sync, hops/shakes/entrances and moving camera — not keyframed acting).
- AI image or AI video generation for the videos (rejected: Gemini image models aren't on the free tier;
  FLUX can't keep characters consistent; AI video isn't free).
- Automating the channel name / handle / avatar change (the YouTube API can't; owner does it by hand).

**Anti-goals (a failure even if it runs)**
- A clone: reusing Ricks Clips' character designs, their exact stories/titles, or audio. Topics may
  overlap in subject (pirates, Japan…) but the angle, script and scenes must be our own.
- Slideshow feel: static images with captions. Every second needs motion (camera and/or characters/FX).
- Made-up facts, unverifiable "facts", or fake sources.
- Templated sameness that triggers YouTube's "inauthentic content" policy: identical structure, same set
  and same shots every day.
- Any paid API or a bill. Everything stays free-tier.
- Copyright claims: only YouTube Audio Library tracks the owner downloaded; no publisher images.

## 4. Content

**Topic space:** odd history, hidden engineering, everyday science, psychology/brain, strange laws, jobs
and customs, luck/gambling mechanics, "what if" thought experiments, "Levels of X" escalations. Each must
have a surprising, true, checkable answer.

**Title formats** (≤ 60 chars, curiosity without lying), mirroring what performs in the reference:
"Why X…", "How X…", "The X That Y", "This X Saved Y", "Levels of X", "What If…", "The Job/Law/Rule…".
Reference titles for tone: Why Sailors Carried Storm Oil · What the Grave Bell Really Meant · The Burglar
Who Only Moved Furniture · Why Perfect Railroads Don't Work · This Shape Saved Roller Coasters.

**Story shape** (45–60 s, ~130–170 spoken words, fast delivery):
1. Hook (0–2 s): a question or impossible-sounding claim, characters already on screen and moving.
2. Setup: the situation/problem.
3. Turn(s): the surprising mechanism or story; for "Levels of", 4–7 levels, each nastier/bigger, each
   with its own set/lighting change.
4. Payoff: answers the hook; last line ties back to it (good for loops). No outro, no "subscribe" card,
   no spoken sign-off.

**Topic supply (owner's choice: automatic):** Gemini proposes topics into a backlog file
(`topics/queue`-style) and the daily run takes the next unused one. Dedupe by normalized topic and by
similarity to past titles (history). Builder decides the exact backlog format and refill trigger; keep a
category rotation so consecutive videos differ (e.g. no two "Levels of" or two pirate topics in a row).

**Research and fact safety:** Gemini with Google Search grounding researches the topic and returns the
script plus source URLs (Odd Fortune's `generate_story.py` pattern). A second Gemini pass fact-checks the
script against the gathered sources; failure → retry once with a new draft, then skip to the next topic.
Sources go in the description. Owner accepted the YPP risk of no human review; these checks stand in for it.

## 5. Visual design (from the reference video)

**Format:** 1080×1920, 30 fps (reference is 720×1280; we render higher). Toon-shaded bright 3D world with
flat 2D characters standing in it.

**Characters (Gus world cast):**
- Body: large **white round head** with a thick dark ink outline, **dot eyes**, simple line mouth/brows,
  thin **stick limbs**, small rounded torso that carries the costume. Must read as our own design, not
  Ricks Clips' (proportions, Gus's signature item and palette differ — builder's call within this look).
- Expressive faces are the main acting tool: neutral, smug, angry, shocked, scared (with sweat drop),
  squint-pain (>_<), happy, sad. Lip-sync while talking.
- Costumes by role via a look map (Odd Fortune's `cast` idea): bandana + striped shirt (sailor/pirate),
  coat + hat (officer/navy), lab coat, suit, worker, king, knight, etc.
- **Gus**: recurring narrator mascot with a fixed signature look (builder designs; same in avatar,
  banner and videos). Appears in most videos, often in the hook or payoff.
- Objects can get faces for comic beats (the reference's scared powder bag).
- Motion: idle bob, talk bob, poses, hop, shake/flinch, run, enter from side/drop in, fall over.

**Sets / world:** painted-look 3D environments, rounded chunky shapes, soft textures, saturated palette
(warm wood browns, teal-blue sea, sky blue, green jungle tops). Built from parametric pieces so many
topics can be staged: e.g. ship deck, open sea, harbour/fort, workshop/furnace, street/town, room/office,
lab, field/countryside, castle, abstract "inside view" (cut-away of an object). The set library grows as
topics need it; the validator must reject stories that ask for sets/props that don't exist (Odd Fortune
pattern).

**Lighting moods** change per beat for drama: day, warm interior glow (furnace orange), sunset
(purple-orange), night, steel/grey. Fire glow lights nearby objects.

**Camera (the most important part):** one continuously moving camera, very few hard cuts (reference: 2
cuts in 63 s). Moves: slow push-in, pull-out, pan, orbit, low-angle along a deck, rise/fly-over to a wide
sea shot, follow a flying object, tilt up a tall object, POV down a cannon barrel, close-up on a face for
a reaction. Each beat continues from the last camera position unless `cut`. Low angles and depth
(foreground props) as in the reference.

**Transitions:** mostly camera moves between subjects in the same world; a **circle-mask iris** zoom for
"next level" moments; occasional hard cut.

**FX:** smoke puffs, fire/flames, splinters/debris, sparks, explosions (cartoon puffy orange/black),
water splash, steam, glowing heat, impact flash. Plus "Levels of" level cards are **not** big text
overlays — the reference shows labels only as in-world signs (barrel labels "LANGRAGE", "CARCASS / KEEP
FROM FLAME", a "HOT SHOT" sign). Use in-world signs/labels for names and numbers.

**Captions:** one word at a time, centred horizontally at about 58% of frame height, white, heavy
rounded sans with a dark outline + soft shadow, ~70 px at 1080 width, no background box, no colour
highlight. Font: a free OFL font in that style (builder picks; not Lilita One, to avoid Odd Fortune's look).

**Audio:** voice up front; music bed from `music/` under it (ducked, ~-20 dB under voice); SFX for whoosh,
cannon boom, splash, impact, pop (code-generated like Odd Fortune's `audio.py`, or free/own). Loudness
-14 LUFS.

## 6. Production pipeline (decided parts)

```
topic backlog → Gemini research + story JSON (grounded, sources) → validator → fact-check
→ narration (edge-tts, word timings; Kokoro as free fallback) → beat timeline
→ Three.js engine renders frames in headless Chromium → ffmpeg (video + voice + music + SFX, -14 LUFS)
→ YouTube Short + Facebook Reel (existing youtube.py / facebook.py) → history.json
```

- **Story format:** Odd Fortune's beat JSON is the starting point (text, set, keep, objects, chars,
  labels, fx, cam) extended for this design (lighting mood, iris transition, new FX, follow-cam,
  object faces). Builder finalizes the schema.
- **Voice:** edge-tts (as now) — designer's call: a fast, energetic US male narrator like the
  reference; builder picks the voice/rate and fallback (Kokoro local or existing Piper fallback).
- **Music:** YouTube Audio Library tracks the owner downloads into `music/` with a small manifest per
  track (title, artist, attribution line if required, "facebook ok" flag). Attribution lines go in the
  YouTube description automatically. Tracks not marked OK for Facebook are not used in the Reel
  version (Reel then gets the same video with a Facebook-safe track or no music — builder's call).
  The owner should prefer "no attribution required" tracks.
- **Render time:** Odd Fortune takes ~15–20 min per 50 s Short on GitHub's 2-core CPU runner. So the
  timer starts each edition early enough to publish near the slot time (e.g. trigger ~40 min before
  17:00 / 23:00 UTC; builder may instead render ahead and upload at the slot). Job timeout must cover a
  slow render.
- **GitHub Actions minutes:** 2 renders/day ≈ 1,800–2,400 min/month. The repo will be public
  (unlimited minutes; see §8a).
- **Reused from the current repo:** `autopilot/youtube.py` (upload, metadata), `autopilot/facebook.py`
  (publish_reel), `autopilot/history.py` (ids, dedupe, once-per-slot guard), `autopilot/voice.py`
  (edge-tts + word timings + fallback), `autopilot/llm.py` (Gemini client with model fallback),
  the workflow's timed/manual/backup-cron structure and history save step, `setup_cronjobs.py`,
  `get_token.py`. Shared narration rule ideas in `rules.py` that still apply (no first-person filler,
  disclosure line).
- **Removed:** everything crypto (sources, focus, slate, script/slides/thumbnail market code, news_post,
  images, triggers + `triggers.yml`, pulse, recap, triggered, basics, lessons, detectors, material,
  diagrams, seo market logic), their config sections, tests and data files, and the retired cron-job.org
  jobs. CLAUDE.md and README rewritten for Gus Explains.
- **Editions:** `short_a` due 17:00 UTC, `short_b` due 23:00 UTC, every day; same timer model as now
  (cron-job.org primary, GitHub cron backup, late window, once-per-slot guard, manual runs).

**Metadata:** title (format rules above), description = one-line hook, 2–3 sentence summary, "Sources:"
list, music attribution if needed, disclosure line ("Narrated with a synthetic voice." — AI-voice
disclosure), 3–5 hashtags (#Shorts last on YouTube; none on Facebook). Category 27 (Education).
`containsSyntheticMedia` per current policy for code-animated cartoons with synthetic voice
(builder checks current YouTube guidance). Reel caption built from the same metadata.

**Thumbnail:** Shorts thumbnails can't be set via API for this channel, so frame 0 must be a strong
image: the hook scene with characters mid-action and the title's key word as an in-world sign or
caption. Also used as the Facebook Reel cover.

## 8a. Cutover and release (owner decisions, 2026-11-12)

- **Crypto stops now.** The first build step pauses all crypto publishing: disable the cron-job.org jobs
  (or point them nowhere) and the GitHub backup crons / trigger poll, so nothing crypto publishes during
  the build. The channel stays quiet until Gus launches; the owner accepts the gap.
- **Gus waits for the rebrand.** Gus Shorts don't publish on timed runs until the owner has renamed the
  channel and Page and uploaded the new artwork: a config switch (e.g. `gus.enabled: false` → timed runs
  log and exit 0) that the owner flips after the rebrand. Manual `--no-upload` runs work meanwhile for
  checking renders.
- Release order: pause crypto → build (on a branch) → owner rebrands with the generated artwork →
  (optional, separately confirmed) delete old content → merge, create the 17:00/23:00 cron-job.org jobs,
  delete the retired ones → flip the switch → watch the first day's runs.
- **Repo goes public** for unlimited Actions minutes. Before the owner flips visibility, the builder
  checks the working tree and git history for anything sensitive (tokens, `client_secret.json`, personal
  data) and reports what it found; the owner makes the change in GitHub settings.

## 11. Improvement round after the owner's sample review (2026-11-13)

The owner watched the first sample ("Why Lighthouses Flash in Patterns") and found the hook weak and the
script, animation and camera below the reference. Side-by-side analysis of the first 8 s:

| | Reference | Sample v1 |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | "If your ship got hit by a cannonball, you'd actually be one of the lucky ones." — 2nd person, danger, twist | plain fact, no stakes |
| Pace | a new shot / visual event every 0.5–1.5 s | one shot ≈ 6 s |
| Action on words | "hit by" → cannon fires; "cannonball" → camera rides the ball; "smash" → splinters burst | nothing tied to words |
| Camera | close, low, wide lens, tilted, whip-pans, foreground objects | flat, far, level, hard cuts |
| Characters | 40–70 % of frame height, acting with props | ≈ 30 %, standing |
| Light | warm bright day, rich textures | dark blue night tints faces |

Decisions (owner approved 2026-11-13):
1. **Hook & script rules.** Line 1 is a second-person scenario with a twist or high stakes ("If you…, you'd
   actually…", "This X could Y", a surprising number). Then escalation with concrete facts and numbers; a
   visible event every 3–6 spoken words; payoff answers the hook. Topic angles are chosen for "wait, really?"
   stakes, not plain facts.
2. **Shots, not beats.** A story is a fast shot list: each shot 0.6–2.5 s (≈ 2–8 words), 15–40 shots per video.
   Shots carry **events anchored to words** (`at_word` index within the shot): actions, expressions, FX, signs,
   camera shake.
3. **Action library.** Compound animated actions: cannon/gun fire (recoil, muzzle flash, smoke), projectile
   launch on an arc with camera follow, impact bursts (splinters/debris/splash by surface), prop fly-off;
   character actions hold, drink, throw, duck, cower, grab, run_to, point_at, look_at, jump, fall; squash &
   stretch on hits.
4. **Camera v2.** Framing presets relative to a subject (`close`, `medium`, `two_shot`, `ots`, `low_hero`,
   `wide`, `pov`), default wide lens (fov ≈ 60), `roll` (dutch tilt), foreground objects, and **whip-pan
   transitions** (fast rotation + motion blur) that hide set changes; hard cuts become rare.
5. **Look v2.** Characters bigger by default; warm daylight preferred; night scenes moonlit-bright with faces
   staying white (characters not tinted by mood beyond a light rim); richer textures; a lighthouse lens prop
   and a sweeping light-beam FX; sign text rendered as written.
6. The sample is rebuilt with all of the above and shown to the owner again side by side with the reference
   before the automatic writer is built.
Limit, said to the owner: hand-animated frames are not fully reachable by code; this round targets what
viewers notice (hook, pace, action on words, camera energy, character size).
